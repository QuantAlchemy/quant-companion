Stop loss and take profit alerts

Each open trade can have an optional stop loss and take profit. When the price reaches a level, Quant Companion tells you in the app, and also by browser notification or email if you turn those on.

An alert, not an order. Quant Companion never sells or closes anything. A hit gives you one click, "Close at", which records the exit in the journal at the level. Keep your real orders with your broker.

Decided on November 16. Screen B, the alert inbox. Take profit alerts as well as stops. Checks once an hour, because these are swing trades. Email is in: it uses the Resend account and sending domains Netly already verified, with a plain fetch and no new package.

The screens. The mocks show three directions: in the table, an alert inbox, and a risk board. No code changes until you pick one.

How it works

  1. You set the levels. The trade form gets an optional stop loss, an optional take profit, and an "Alert me" checkbox. The form shows the risk at the stop and the reward at the take profit, and the stop fills in the risk for R. A long hits its stop at or below it and its take profit at or above it. A short is the mirror image.
  2. Convex checks prices every hour. A scheduled job reads the open trades with an armed level. It asks for one price for each symbol: CoinMarketCap for crypto and Alpaca for stocks. With no armed levels, it makes no price calls. This works while the app is closed.
  3. A level fires once. For each hit, one mutation writes an alert and marks the level as hit. A later check does not alert again. Move the level and it is armed again.
  4. You get told. The in-app alert updates live through a Convex query. A browser notification goes through a service worker and Web Push. An email goes through the Convex Resend component.
  5. You decide. "Close at stop" opens the normal close form with the stop price filled in. Change the price to your real fill before you save. "Edit stop" moves the stop and arms it again.

Data changes

TableChange
tradesAdd optional stopPrice, targetPrice, priceAlerts, stopHitAt, and targetHitAt, plus an index on status for the scheduled job. Old trades need no migration.
alerts (new)One row for each hit: user, trade, stop, price seen, and when. It also records when you read it and when the email or notification went out.
alertSettings (new)One row for each user: browser on or off, email on or off. In-app alerts are always on.
pushSubscriptions (new)One row for each browser that allowed notifications.

The MCP tools add_trade and update_trade accept stopPrice and targetPrice. get_trade and list_trades return both levels and when each was hit.

Limits to know

  • Checks every hour. A move through a level that comes back within the hour can be missed. A later version can read hourly bars and use the low or high instead of the last price.
  • Price data cost. One CoinMarketCap call an hour is about 720 calls a month. Alpaca's free data covers stocks through IEX.
  • Extended hours. For stocks, Alpaca's latest trade can be a pre-market or after-hours trade, so a level can fire outside market hours.
  • Keys move. The price keys live on Vercel today. The scheduled job runs in Convex, so the same keys must be set on the Convex deployment.
  • Email needs the email claim. The address comes from the email claim in Clerk's convex JWT template. The development instance had no convex template at all, so the template was created with that claim on November 16. Check that production's template has it.
  • Browser notifications need permission once on each device. On an iPhone they work only after you add the app to the home screen.

What was built and checked

Branch feat/price-alerts, on top of this plan. All 173 tests pass, including 12 new alert tests: the hit rule for both sides and both levels, fire once and arm again, one price call for each symbol, level validation, and the email.

On the development deployment, with real prices: three test levels fired once each, the emails reached Resend's test inbox, and a push to a dead subscription removed it. In a browser, a test user logged a trade with both levels, saw the bell badge and the alert, and "Close at" opened the close dialog at the stop price. The test trades and the test user were deleted.

Still open

  1. Production keys. Production needs the price keys, VAPID keys, a Resend key, ALERT_FROM_EMAIL on mail.labs.quantalchemy.io, and APP_URL. Nothing on production changes until you say so.
  2. A Resend key of its own. Development uses Netly's key. Production should have a key for Quant Companion.
  3. Recording on your real journal. Alerts show real tickers and sizes. Decide which screens you show on camera, or blur sizes and P&L in the edit.
